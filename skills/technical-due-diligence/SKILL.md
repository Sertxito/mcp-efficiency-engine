# Technical due diligence

## Procedure
1. Confirm decision, deadline, system boundary, evidence access, and coverage
   limitations.
2. Inventory architecture, lifecycle, security, operations, data, dependencies,
   owners, and vendor/team concentration.
3. Distinguish verified liabilities from hypotheses and quantify only from
   sourced assumptions.
4. Identify commitment blockers, mitigations, and evidence needed before the
   decision.
5. Return findings and questions to the accountable business/technical owner.

## Decision evidence
Inspect deployment ownership, lifecycle/license obligations, dependency/vendor
concentration, evidence retention, secrets/access handover and operating cost
assumptions. Use system-forensics to connect material findings to real user
journeys, drift and recovery evidence rather than isolated code-style smells.
Record severity, confidence, blast radius, owner, verification and commitment
blocker separately; missing access or an untested restore remains unknown.
Propose incremental stabilization and dated verification gates before making
cost/schedule commitments. A tidy repository is not operational assurance.

## Output
Scope, evidence register, material risks, unknowns, cost hypotheses, and
decision gates. Do not make the investment decision or claim complete assurance.
